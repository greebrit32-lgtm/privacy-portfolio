# Project 02 — Privacy Impact Assessment & Data Map: "Lantern Local"

**Scenario:** I'm the privacy analyst at **Mesquite Lantern Co.**, a fictional Texas startup about to launch **Lantern Local**, a free, ad-supported app for finding local events (adults 18+). Before launch, I mapped every type of personal data the app handles, traced where it goes, and assessed the privacy risks of the design ([scenario](scenario.md)).

**Measured against:** the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Ch. 541, including its 2026 amendment), the Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act (effective January 2026), FTC Act §5 and the FTC's 2024 location-data actions, and the Fair Information Practice Principles (FIPPs).

**Status:** Complete (November 2026). Recommendation: **launch with conditions.**

## What I found

- **The design reused location and sensitive interests for advertising.** Precise location, plus event categories that can reveal religion, sexuality, or health, flowed into profiles, ad requests, and audience segments sold to advertisers as "anonymous." Under Texas law these are "sensitive data" that require specific consent, and hashed device IDs aren't de-identified. This matches the pattern in the FTC's 2024 InMarket case.
- **Four design changes fixed most of it:**
  - contextual ads only, with no segment sales;
  - background location opt-in and geofenced;
  - 7-day raw location retention instead of 24 months;
  - sensitive categories search-only.
  
  Together they took the risk from **8 High to 0 High** (14 risks; residual 3 Medium, 11 Low).
- **A new Texas AI law came into scope.** While checking the statute's currency, I found a 2025 amendment that led to the Texas Responsible AI Governance Act. The app's recommendation engine likely counts as an "AI system." The Act's private-sector rules are intent-based, so the practical step is keeping AI documentation ready.

## How the work was done

1. **Scenario and sources** ([scenario](scenario.md), [source register](sources/source-register.md)): the fictional company and app, Texas statutes as enacted, current text checked on the official statutes site, FTC releases, and FIPPs.
2. **System description** ([working/system-description.md](working/system-description.md)): the product team's design *before* review, written in data **types** only.
3. **Data inventory** ([.xlsx](deliverables/1-data-inventory.xlsx), generated by [`tools/build_inventory.py`](../tools/build_inventory.py)): sensitivity, Texas category, assessment triggers, consent basis, and FIPPs for each data type.
4. **Data-flow map** ([map](deliverables/2-data-flow-map.md), generated by [`tools/build_dataflow.py`](../tools/build_dataflow.py) from the same files, so map and inventory can't disagree). Building it surfaced flows the inventory had missed, and those were fixed at the source.
5. **Risks** ([register](working/risk-register.md), [AI Act assessment](working/traiga-assessment.md)): 14 risks, each cited. Rated likelihood × impact before and after the design changes.
6. **Report and summary:** the PIA report follows §541.105(b) (benefits vs. risks, de-identified data, consumer expectations, context, relationship). The executive summary states the business trade-off of each change.

## Ground rules I followed
- **Fictional organization, fictional product, data by type only.** No records, real or synthetic.
- **Every legal claim is cited** to a source ID and section, and quotes were checked against saved source text. Statute currency was confirmed on the official Texas site, which is how the 2026 amendment was found.
- **Neutral wording:** findings state the requirement and the "gap relative to" it. FTC releases are labeled as allegations and orders in other cases.
- **Division of labor:** an AI assistant (Claude) helped research and draft. I chose the scenario and scope and made every judgment call: sensitivity, risk ratings, the four design decisions, and the launch recommendation. The [session log](session-log.md) records each decision.
