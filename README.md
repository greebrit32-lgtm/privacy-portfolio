# Privacy Analyst Portfolio — Brittany Green

After 14 years as a high school teacher, I'm moving into privacy and compliance. I hold **CompTIA Security+ (SY0-701)** and **Microsoft SC-300 (Identity and Access Administrator)**, and I'm studying for the **IAPP CIPP/US**. In the classroom I was the person handling student data every day. This portfolio shows the work on the other side: reviewing vendors, mapping data flows, and getting ready for incidents.

Each project copies a task an entry-level Privacy Analyst does at work. All of them use **public information and fictional organizations only**.

## Projects

| # | Project | What it shows | Status |
|---|---------|---------------|--------|
| 01 | [EdTech Vendor Privacy Review](01-edtech-vendor-review/) | Third-party risk assessment of a real edtech vendor's public documents (Wayground, formerly Quizizz) against FERPA, COPPA, Texas student-data law, and the SDPC National Data Privacy Agreement. Deliverables: questionnaire, risk assessment, and a 2-page memo recommending conditional approval. | **Complete** |
| 02 | [Privacy Impact Assessment & Data Map](02-pia-data-map/) | Pre-launch PIA of a fictional Texas events app that uses location and advertising: data inventory, data-flow map, a PIA structured on the Texas data protection assessment requirements, and a 1-page summary. Four design changes took risk from 8 High to 0 High. Includes the Texas AI Governance Act (2026). | **Complete** |
| 03 | [Incident Response Tabletop](03-incident-tabletop/) | Ransomware incident at a fictional Texas health clinic, measured against HIPAA breach notification, Texas breach law, and NIST SP 800-61r3. Done so far: incident response playbook (roles, severity levels, notification decision tree), a breach-notification requirements matrix with a worked deadline example, and a tabletop exercise package. Live tabletop exercise underway. | **In progress** |

## Skills demonstrated
- **Vendor / third-party privacy risk assessment:** questionnaire design, a weighted scoring rubric, and likelihood × impact risk ratings *(Project 01)*
- **Reading law and contracts closely:** FERPA's school-official exception, the COPPA Rule and its 2025 amendments, Texas Education Code Ch. 32 Subch. D, and the SDPC NDPA / TX-NDPA *(Project 01)*
- **Evidence and sourcing discipline:** every claim cited to a dated source and section, with archived versions compared over time *(Project 01)*
- **Writing for decision-makers:** a 2-page memo with a clear recommendation and contract conditions *(Project 01)*
- **Data mapping and privacy by design:** a data inventory and a generated data-flow map; design changes that reduce risk while the business trade-offs are stated openly *(Project 02)*
- **State consumer privacy and AI law:** the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (sensitive data, consent, dark patterns, sale and targeted advertising, assessments) and the Texas Responsible AI Governance Act *(Project 02)*
- **Keeping legal research current:** statute history checked on official sites, which found a 2026 amendment that changed the scope *(Projects 01–02)*
- **Privacy in my own practice:** fictional data only, and no republishing of personal data found in public records *(Project 01)*
