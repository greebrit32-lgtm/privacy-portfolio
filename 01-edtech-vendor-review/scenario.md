# Scenario — Pecan Hollow ISD

> **FICTIONAL ORGANIZATION.** Pecan Hollow Independent School District is invented for this exercise. Every detail about the district (enrollment, campuses, staff, requests, data) is made up for illustration. On 2026-11-03, no Texas school district with this name was found on Wikipedia's [List of school districts in Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Texas) or in a web search.

## 2. The request

A group of teachers from all three school levels has asked the district to approve **Wayground** for classroom use across the district. Wayground is a quiz, lesson, and assessment platform. Per the project brief, it was formerly called Quizizz; S3 will confirm this with a vendor source. Teachers want to use it for formative assessment, homework practice, and test review.

District procedure says a new app that collects student data must get a privacy review before it goes on the **Approved Digital Resources** list. The District Privacy Officer has asked the analyst for:

1. A completed vendor privacy questionnaire with scores
2. A risk assessment with a findings table
3. A memo of no more than 2 pages recommending **approve**, **conditionally approve**, or **reject**, plus the contract terms the district should require

## 4. Data the district expects to share (district assumption)

This is what PHISD *expects* to happen if teachers use the platform. It is **not** a statement of what Wayground collects. S8–S9 will compare it with the vendor's documents.

- **Roster data:** student name, district email, grade level, and class or section, through Google sign-in or a roster sync
- **Student work:** quiz and assignment responses, scores, completion times, and any text students type in open-ended answers
- **Teacher data:** name, district email, school, and content the teachers create
- **Device and usage data:** collected automatically, such as browser, IP address, and activity logs. The vendor's documents should say what is collected.

**Not intended to be shared:** special education records, health information, discipline records, Social Security numbers, and home addresses.

## 5. Features in scope

- **Core platform:** quizzes, lessons, assignments, reports, and classroom games
- **AI features:** the AI features the project brief says launched with the 2025 rebrand, such as AI-generated questions or content. S3 will list them by name and with sources. The main question is whether the public privacy documents **specifically cover** these features: what data goes into them, whether any third-party AI provider receives student data, and whether student data is used to train models.
- **Legacy documents and agreements:** whether privacy policies, terms, student-privacy pledges, or data privacy agreements signed under the **Quizizz** name still apply to Wayground. This includes any Texas or SDPC agreements found in a public registry. Checked in S3.

## 6. Out of scope

- Anything not available in public documents. No requests to the vendor, no account sign-up to inspect data collection, and no requests for non-public contracts.
- Technical or security testing of the platform
- Negotiating a contract with the vendor. The memo only *recommends* terms.
- Other apps the district uses

## 7. Review standards

Named here only. Each one will be registered with a URL and access date in S4.

- **FERPA**, including the school-official exception for vendors
- **COPPA**, including how schools can authorize collection from students under 13
- **Texas Education Code Chapter 32, Subchapter D** (student data privacy for operators)
- **Student Data Privacy Consortium (SDPC) National Data Privacy Agreement (NDPA)**, used as a benchmark for contract terms
