| 2026-11-03 | Who counts as a "child" under COPPA, and when a school can authorize collection for students | COPPA — verifiable parental consent; school authorization | _Brittany to write in own words._ **Sourced facts:** "Child means an individual under the age of 13" (16 CFR §312.2 [S17]). Operators need verifiable parental consent before collecting a child's personal information (§312.5(a)(1) [S17]). The rule text never mentions schools. The FTC's guidance says a school may consent instead of the parent only in the educational context, and only for "the use and benefit of the school, and for no other commercial purpose" (FTC COPPA FAQ §N.1–N.2 [S19]). The FTC recommends that the school or district make that decision, not individual teachers (§N.3 [S19]). Rule amended effective 2025-06-23 [S18]. Why it matters here: PHISD's PK–8 students are mostly under 13. | scenario.md §3 |
| 2026-11-03 | When a vendor can count as a "school official" and receive student records without parent consent | FERPA — education records & the school-official exception | _Brittany to write in own words._ **Sourced facts:** An outside party can count as a "school official" if it (1) performs a service the school would otherwise use employees for, (2) "is under the direct control of the agency or institution with respect to the use and maintenance of education records," and (3) follows the redisclosure limits in §99.33(a) (34 CFR §99.31(a)(1)(i)(B) [S16]). The school must use "reasonable methods" to limit access to records with a legitimate educational interest (§99.31(a)(1)(ii) [S16]). Why it matters here: if Wayground gets roster data and student work, the district needs a FERPA basis for sharing them, and a contract (DPA) is how a district shows "direct control." | scenario.md §4 |
| 2026-11-03 | Privacy policy vs. data privacy agreement (DPA): a policy is the vendor's one-sided notice and can change whenever the vendor updates it. A DPA is a signed contract with the district that both sides must agree to change. | Information management — vendor/third-party management; contracts. FERPA — "direct control" condition of the school-official exception (34 CFR §99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)(2) [S16]) | _Brittany to write in own words._ Why it matters here: S01 was revised June 9, 2026, but the Texas DPA (S12) was signed in June 2025 under the Quizizz name. | sources S01, S06, S12 |
| 2026-11-03 | Regulation vs. agency guidance: a regulation (CFR) is binding law. Guidance (FAQs, PTAC documents) explains how the agency reads the law. COPPA's school-consent approach exists only in guidance. | Legal framework — sources of U.S. privacy law; FTC enforcement | _Brittany to write in own words._ Why it matters here: a finding can cite "gap relative to FTC guidance" but should not call that guidance a legal requirement. | S17 vs. S19 |
